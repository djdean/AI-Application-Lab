## 📄 Document 1

### *Department of Justice – New Process for Student Loan Bankruptcy Discharge*



### 🔹 Purpose & Background

* Student loans are treated differently in bankruptcy.
* To discharge student loans, borrowers must prove **“undue hardship”** in a separate **adversary proceeding**.
* Historically, this process has been difficult and inconsistent.
* DOJ + Department of Education introduced a **new standardized process** to:

  * Improve transparency
  * Reduce debtor burden
  * Increase consistency
  * Allow DOJ attorneys to **support discharge** when appropriate

---

### 🔹 New DOJ Process (High-Level)

* Borrowers complete an **attestation form**
* DOJ + Department of Education jointly review the information
* DOJ evaluates whether to:

  * Support **full discharge**
  * Support **partial discharge**
  * Oppose discharge
* **Final decision still belongs to the bankruptcy judge**

---

### 🔹 Undue Hardship Evaluation Framework

The DOJ evaluates **three factors**, aligned with court standards:

---

#### 1️⃣ Present Ability to Pay

* DOJ uses **IRS expense standards**
* Compares:

  * Debtor income
  * Necessary living expenses
* If expenses ≥ income → **No present ability to pay**

---

#### 2️⃣ Future Ability to Pay

DOJ assesses whether the inability to pay is **likely to persist**.

A **presumption of continued hardship** applies if factors such as:

* Retirement age
* Disability or chronic injury
* Long-term unemployment
* Lack of degree
* Extended repayment history
  are present.

If not, DOJ evaluates evidence case-by-case.

---

#### 3️⃣ Good Faith Efforts

Focuses on **objective behavior**, not punishment.

DOJ considers:

* Efforts to earn income
* Expense management
* Attempts to repay or seek repayment options

Important clarifications:

* ❌ Past non-payment alone does **not** disqualify a borrower
* ❌ Failure to enroll in Income-Driven Repayment (IDR) does **not** automatically disqualify
* ✅ Reasonable explanations for non-enrollment are accepted
* ✅ Contacting loan servicers or DOE weighs positively

## 📘 Document 2

### *Student Loan Guide – Federal vs Private Loans (Credible)*



---

## 🔹 Key Takeaways

* **Federal loans should always be applied for first**
* Private loans should only be used **after federal aid is maximized**
* About **30% of adults** who pursued higher education borrowed money

---

## 🔹 Federal vs Private Student Loans (Core Differences)

| Category         | Federal Loans                | Private Loans                        |
| ---------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Lender           | U.S. Department of Education | Banks, credit unions, online lenders |
| Interest Rates   | Fixed (6.53%–9.08%)          | Fixed or variable (3.39%–17.99%)     |
| Credit Check     | ❌ Not required (most)        | ✅ Required                           |
| Borrowing Limits | Capped by academic level     | Up to cost of attendance             |
| Repayment        | 10–25 years                  | 5–20 years                           |
| Protections      | IDR, deferment, forgiveness  | Very limited                         |
| Forgiveness      | Possible                     | None                                 |

---

## 🔹 Federal Student Loan Eligibility

To qualify, a borrower must:

* Be a U.S. citizen or eligible non-citizen
* Have a valid SSN
* Be enrolled in an eligible degree or certificate program
* Maintain satisfactory academic progress
* Have a high school diploma or equivalent
* Not be in default on federal aid
* Submit FAFSA annually

⚠️ **No income cutoff** exists for FAFSA eligibility.

---

## 🔹 FAFSA Process (Federal Loans)

1. Create **FSA ID**
2. Complete FAFSA (opens ~Oct 1 annually)
3. Sign & submit FAFSA
4. Review financial aid award letter
5. Accept aid (grants → subsidized → unsubsidized → PLUS)
6. Complete entrance counseling & promissory note (first-time borrowers)

---

## 🔹 Types of Federal Aid

### Need-Based

* Pell Grants (no repayment)
* Direct Subsidized Loans
* FSEOG grants
* Federal Work-Study

### Non–Need-Based

* Direct Unsubsidized Loans
* Direct PLUS Loans (credit check required)

---

## 🔹 Private Student Loans

### Eligibility Criteria

* U.S. citizen or permanent resident
* SSN
* Age 18+
* Good credit score (often ~670+)
* Minimum income (or cosigner)
* Enrollment at least half-time

### Key Characteristics

* Credit & income heavily weighted
* Often require a cosigner
* No forgiveness programs
* Fewer hardship protections
* Should be **last-resort financing**

---

## 🔹 Private Loan Evaluation Factors

* Credit score & history
* Income
* Debt-to-income (DTI) ratio
* School & program eligibility
* Loan amount and term length

---

## 🔹 Application & Disbursement

* Funds are sent **directly to the school**
* Any excess funds go to the borrower for qualified expenses
* Denials must include reason
* Appeals or cosigners may be options
